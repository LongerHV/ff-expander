# ff-expander — hardware

Companion to `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-23-ff-expander-design.md`.

KiCad schematics: `hardware/rx/` and `hardware/tx/`. They are generated by
`hardware/gen_schematics.py` (edit the script, not the `.kicad_sch` files):

```sh
cd hardware
nix shell nixpkgs#kicad-small nixpkgs#freerouting -c sh -c '
  python3 gen_footprints.py      # our XIAO footprint
  python3 gen_schematics.py      # rx/ and tx/ schematics
  ./kicad-python.sh gen_pcb.py     # both boards: place, autoroute, pour, DRC -> */drc.rpt
'
```

Freerouting is not deterministic, so each PCB run gives a slightly different
routing. Commit the result you like; after hand-editing the board in KiCad,
stop regenerating it.

## TX PCB

2-layer, 52 × 32 mm, M2 holes (2.2 mm) on a 45 × 25 mm pattern.

- XIAO soldered flat, USB-C overhanging the left short edge (charging and
  flashing from outside).
- **Battery pads:** the XIAO's BAT pads face the PCB, over a slot. Assembly
  order (also printed on the back silkscreen):
  1. On the bench, solder two short thin wires (~30 AWG, ~15 mm) to the
     XIAO's BAT+ and BAT− pads.
  2. Thread them down through the slot and solder the XIAO's castellated edges.
  3. On the back, solder the wires to the 2 × 2 mm pads next to the slot
     (+ = VBAT, − = GND) and trim.
- **Antenna:** no copper on either layer under the XIAO's antenna end or for
  4 mm past it, and no top-layer copper under the module body.
- Button rows B0–B9 (10k pull-up, 10 nF, 1k series) next to J2 (11-pin XH,
  pin 1 = button 0 at the bottom, GND at the top). Battery J1 (2-pin XH) at
  the right edge.
- The battery-sense divider (R21/R22/C11, not fitted) is on the bottom, under
  the XIAO.

## RX PCB

2-layer, 54 × 31 mm. M2 holes (2.2 mm): two in the left corners, two at
(44.4, 3) / (44.4, 28) mm, just inboard of the connector column.

- XIAO soldered flat, USB-C overhanging the left short edge; same keep-outs as
  TX. No battery slot (footprint and symbol variant `_NoBat`).
- Rows B0–B9: 1k series + 10k pull-up per button, ordered to match the XIAO
  pin order. The two 74HC165s are stacked next to them (U2 = buttons 8/9 on
  top, U1 below), with their 100 nF caps between them.
- Both XDRIVE connectors on the right edge: J1 (6-pin XH, pin 1 = GND at the
  bottom, `GND MOSI MISO SCK GPIO6 3V3` printed on the back) and J2 (2-pin XH,
  pin 1 = 5V). D1, C1 and C2 sit next to J2.

Known constraints:

- The XDRIVE button header is `[GND MOSI MISO SCK GPIO_6 3.3V]`, and its SPI
  bus is **not shared**. 5 V is available on a separate XDRIVE connector.
- Both enclosures are **3D-printed** (no metal), so both boards use the
  on-board ceramic antennas.
- **2-layer PCBs, hand soldered:** 0805 parts with hand-solder pads, SOIC-16,
  SMA diode, and **JST XH** connectors (note: XH pitch is 2.50 mm).

## RX (base): XIAO → 2× 74HC165 → XDRIVE

```
                         +3V3_XD  (XDRIVE header 3.3 V)
                            │
                     10k ×10 pull-ups (or 9-pin 10k network)
                            │
XIAO out n ──[1k]───────────┴──► 165 parallel input n

U1 (74HC165): A–H = buttons 0–7     U2 (74HC165): A–B = buttons 8–9
U2 C–H ──► +3V3_XD                  U2 SER (pin 10) ──► +3V3_XD
U2 QH (pin 9) ──► U1 SER (pin 10)
U1 QH (pin 9) ──[47Ω]──► XDRIVE MISO
XDRIVE SCK    ──► CLK    (pin 2) of U1 and U2
XDRIVE GPIO_6 ──► SH/LD̅  (pin 1) of U1 and U2   (the latch)
XDRIVE MOSI   ── not connected
CLK INH (pin 15) ──► GND on U1 and U2
VCC pin 16 / GND pin 8; 100 nF at each chip; 10 µF across +3V3_XD on the board
XIAO GND ── XDRIVE GND (ground wire runs with the signal wires)
```

Why each part is there:

- **10k pull-ups to the XDRIVE rail:** while the ESP is resetting, being
  flashed, crashed or unpowered, its pins float. The pull-ups make every input
  read "released" (high). This is the failsafe in hardware.
- **165s powered from the XDRIVE, not the XIAO:** if the XIAO dies, the
  XDRIVE still reads valid chips showing "all released", not floating garbage.
- **1k series resistors (ESP → 165):** the two boards are powered separately.
  If the XIAO runs (e.g. USB while flashing) with the base off, the ESP would
  otherwise drive the unpowered 165s and XDRIVE rail through their input clamp
  diodes. 1k limits that to ~3 mA per pin. Pressed level with 10k/1k ≈ 0.3 V,
  well below 74HC V_IL at 3.3 V.
- **47 Ω on MISO:** damps ringing on the cable; place it at the 165 end.
- **Latch wiring:** a 74HC165 keeps loading while SH/LD̅ is low, so GPIO_6
  must be a latch pulse, not a CS held low for the whole transfer. Confirm in
  the ffbeast config. Verify the bit order and button numbering on the bench
  (QH = input H shifts out first).
- 74HC165 at 3.3 V is good to roughly 15 MHz of shift clock. If the XDRIVE
  clocks faster, use SN74LV165A (same pinout).

### RX power

The XIAO (radio always on: 80–100 mA average, ~300 mA TX peaks) runs from the
XDRIVE's **5 V connector**, never from the 3.3 V header pin:

XDRIVE 5 V (J2, JST XH 2-pin) → **SS14 Schottky** → XIAO `5V` pin, with
22 µF + 100 nF there.

- The diode stops back-feeding the PC when USB is plugged in while the base
  is on.
- Check that the XDRIVE's 5 V rail can spare ~300 mA peaks.
- Match J2's pin order to the XDRIVE connector.

## TX (wheel): XIAO + LiPo + 10 buttons

Per button (D1–D10):

```
3V3 ──10k──┬── GPIO
           ├── 10 nF ── GND
           └──1k── wire ── button ── GND
```

- **10k external pull-up:** the internal one (~45k) is weak. The wires run
  next to the FFB motor, and the eager debounce acts on the first edge, so
  noise would show up as phantom presses. Current only flows while a button
  is held, so this costs nothing in deep sleep. It also keeps the D1/D2 wake
  pins defined without holding the internal pull-ups during sleep.
- **10 nF:** filters spikes (τ ≈ 100 µs, invisible at a 1 kHz scan).
- **1k series:** limits ESD from fingers. It also protects D6 (GPIO16 =
  UART0 TX, which the boot ROM drives) if that button is held during boot.

Power:

- **Battery:** 1000 mAh LiPo **with a protection circuit**, plugged into a
  JST XH 2-pin (J1). It is wired to the BAT+/BAT− pads on the XIAO's
  underside. Check the polarity — lead colours aren't standard. Most packs
  ship with a JST PH plug, so re-crimp or adapt it.
- **Soldering the battery pads:** they sit under the module. On the PCB, give
  pads 23/24 plated holes so they can be soldered from the back with an iron.
- **No power switch:** after 10 min idle the TX goes to deep sleep (~15 µA).
  Unplug J1 for storage.
- **Charging:** the on-board charger runs at ~100 mA, so a full charge takes
  many hours.
- **Battery sense (future, D0):** 470k / 470k divider plus 100 nF on the ADC
  node. 4.2 V reads as 2.1 V at ~4 µA continuous draw.

## Pin-map notes for firmware

1. **RX: move output 6 from D6 to D10.** D6 = GPIO16 = UART0 TX, which the boot
   ROM drives at every reset regardless of console config, so button 6 would
   flicker pressed on every boot. New RX map:
   `{0, 1, 2, 21, 22, 23, 18, 17, 19, 20}` (D0–D5, D10, D7–D9). D6 is unused.
2. **RX:** set each output level *before* switching the pin to output.
3. **TX:** D6 stays usable thanks to the 1k series resistor. Alternative:
   permanently disable ROM logging via eFuse.
4. No D pins are strapping pins.
